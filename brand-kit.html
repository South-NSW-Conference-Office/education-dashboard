Dashboard brand & UI asset kit

A reference client sent us a dashboard mock-up as a layout benchmark — stat tiles with trend chips, a revenue bar chart, a category donut, a filterable order table, dark sidebar navigation. This kit takes that structure and rebuilds every component in the SNSW palette already live in the finance and databoard app, so the pattern language is consistent everywhere the dashboard grows next. Chrome and cards now sit on translucent, blurred glass over a soft brand-tinted backdrop; primary buttons and status colours stay fully opaque so actions and health states never lose contrast.

Colour

The reference mock used an indigo/purple accent — that hue belongs to the template, not to SNSW. Spacing, elevation & glass

One 4px-based scale for gaps and padding; one radius per surface role; a single soft shadow used only where a card floats above the page canvas; and for glass surfaces, an opacity floor per role so text never depends on the blur for contrast.

Card shadow — 0 8px 28px rgba(31,41,64,.12) plus a 1px inner top highlight. Applied once, at the card boundary. Nested elements never carry their own shadow or blur.

Glass surfaces — opacity floors

The blurred backdrop is atmosphere, not contrast. Every fill below has to pass its text-contrast target on its own, with the blur switched off — then the blur is free to add depth.

Cards & chrome--glass · white 72% / dark 66%Blur 18px · body text ≥ 7:1, secondary ≥ 4.5:1
Dense data & controls--glass-strong · white 86% / dark 82%Tables, buttons, swatches, type specimens · small text ≥ 4.5:1
Sidebar--rail-glass · near-black 82–86%Blur 24px · nav text ≥ 7:1 on every backdrop, both modes
Feature tile--accent-glass · brand blue 92%White text ≥ 6:1 · white edge at 42% · own blue shadow
Edge--glass-border · ink 9% / white 12%The 1px edge plus inner highlight is what reads as glass — not the blur
Nestingone blur per levelChildren of a glass surface take a fill only, no backdrop-filter of their own

Do / don't

Do

  • Use SNSW blue as the only interactive accent colour across nav, links and primary actions.
  • Keep green/amber/red exclusive to health and status — never as decoration or category colour.
  • Set financial figures in tabular numerals so columns align.
  • Reserve gold for a single highlight per screen — a callout, one chart series, or one accent button.
  • Raise a glass surface's opacity (keep the blur) behind dense line-item tables, so figures stay legible.

Don't

  • Carry over the reference mock's indigo/purple — it isn't part of this brand.
  • Rainbow a chart legend; tint the single accent instead of introducing new hues.
  • Apply the sidebar's dark surface anywhere outside the sidebar itself.
  • Let a status chip's colour drift from the three-light system for anything else.
  • Stack blurred glass layers on top of each other — one glass surface per level, never nested.
